Global coffee prices went through a major surge in 2025, driven primarily by poor harvests in key producing countries and tight supplies. To understand what happened in 2026, it is important to distinguish the international price of raw coffee beans from supermarket prices for roasted coffee, which can move differently and with a delay.

There are also two major international benchmarks: Arabica, the higher-priced variety commonly used in specialty coffee, and Robusta, widely used in instant coffee and espresso blends. Their prices do not always move together.

### Why prices surged

- Drought and heat in Brazil. Brazil is the world's largest Arabica producer. Adverse weather damaged crop prospects and increased concerns about future supplies. [Reuters, 10 February 2025](https://www.reuters.com/markets/commodities/coffee-new-york-jumps-6-new-record-amid-panic-buying-2025-02-10/).&#x20;

  [image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://www.reuters.com\&sz=32)

  Reuters

- Weather problems in Vietnam. Drought followed by excessive rainfall threatened Robusta production, adding to the global supply squeeze. [Reuters, 4 December 2024](https://www.reuters.com/business/why-are-coffee-prices-trading-near-half-century-highs-2024-12-04/).&#x20;

  [image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://www.reuters.com\&sz=32)

  Reuters

- Tight inventories and reduced export flows. Lower availability meant buyers had less room to absorb crop shortfalls. The ICO's February report documented the price spike alongside declining certified Robusta stocks. [ICO report](https://www.ico.org/documents/cy2024-25/cmr-0225-e.pdf).&#x20;

  [image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://icocoffee.org\&sz=32)

  icocoffee.org

- Market buying amplified the move. Concerns over future availability prompted aggressive buying and record futures prices. Reuters reported Arabica futures above $4.30 per pound on 10 February 2025. [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/markets/commodities/coffee-new-york-jumps-6-new-record-amid-panic-buying-2025-02-10/).&#x20;

Prices remained elevated through much of the year, but the market was volatile rather than rising continuously. By November 2025, the ICO composite indicator averaged 330.44 US cents/lb. It then fell 7.8% month-on-month to 304.68 cents/lb in December as supply concerns eased and export flows strengthened. ### Why prices fell in early 2026

- Improved Brazilian crop outlook. Better rainfall in Minas Gerais eased concerns about future Arabica supplies. Brazil's national supply agency, CONAB, forecast a 2026 crop of 66.2 million bags, up 17.1% year-on-year, in its February outlook. [ICO February 2026 report](https://www.ico.org/documents/cy2025-26/cmr-0226-e.pdf).&#x20;

  [image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://www.ico.org\&sz=32)

  International Coffee Organization

- Better rainfall in Vietnam. Improved growing conditions supported expectations of more available Robusta coffee. [ICO February 2026 report](https://www.ico.org/documents/cy2025-26/cmr-0226-e.pdf).&#x20;

  [image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://www.ico.org\&sz=32)

  International Coffee Organization

- More export supply. Global green-bean exports rose in December 2025 to 10.15 million bags, up 9.2% year-on-year, according to the ICO's January report. [ICO January 2026 report](https://www.ico.org/documents/cy2025-26/cmr-0126-e.pdf).&#x20;

  [image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://www.ico.org\&sz=32)

  International Coffee Organization



The fall was not simply a return to cheap coffee. The ICO noted that certified stocks remained low and near-term physical supply was still relatively tight. In other words, expectations for the coming harvest improved faster than all existing supply constraints disappeared.&#x20;

### The rest of 2026: prices remained volatile

The decline did not continue in a straight line. The ICO's later reports show several reversals:

| April 2026     | 266.24¢/lb       | −2.7%                 |
| August 2026    | 287.29¢/lb       | Essentially unchanged |
| September 2026 | 262.18¢/lb       | −8.7%                 |

Sources: [ICO March report](https://www.ico.org/documents/cy2025-26/cmr-0326-e.pdf), [ICO April price update](https://ico.org/), and [ICO latest market update](https://ico.org/).

The September decline was attributed mainly to increased availability from Brazil's harvest and expectations of ample supply. Low certified Arabica stocks and El Niño uncertainty provided some support, but prices fell across all four ICO coffee groups.&#x20;

## 4. What does this mean for consumers?

Three distinctions matter:

- Wholesale prices fell, but remained high. September 2026's benchmark was about 26% below February 2025's peak, but that does not mean coffee became 26% cheaper in shops.
- Retail prices adjust with a lag. Roasters and retailers may have purchased beans months earlier at higher prices, and packaging, transport, wages and currency exchange rates also affect shelf prices.
- Arabica and Robusta have different price levels. In February 2026, for example, the ICO's Arabica group indicators ranged from 308.62 to 330.89 cents/lb, while Robusta averaged 179.73 cents/lb. [ICO February report](https://www.ico.org/documents/cy2025-26/cmr-0226-e.pdf).&#x20;

  [image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://www.ico.org\&sz=32)

  International Coffee Organization



Bottom line: 2025 was marked by a supply-driven price shock, especially in Arabica. In early 2026, improved harvest expectations and stronger export availability drove a substantial correction. Prices subsequently rebounded at times, but by September the international benchmark had fallen to 262.18 cents/lb. The key variables to watch are Brazil's next harvest, Vietnam's Robusta supply, weather conditions and shipping costs.

Method note: All prices above refer to the ICO Composite Indicator Price (I-CIP), a benchmark for international green-coffee transactions, not a global retail-price index. Monthly averages are used for consistent comparisons.
